# 0002 — Relative-time wording that differs from CLDR

- **Type:** Known differences, kept by decision
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Created:** 2026-10-10

## What this records

The relative-time phrases in `:readable` — "5 minutes ago", "in 3 days" — were compared with Unicode CLDR for every bundled language. Where the library was ungrammatical it was corrected. Where the library and CLDR simply word the same thing differently, and both are valid, the library's wording was left alone. This page lists those remaining differences, so that nobody later mistakes one for a defect, or "fixes" it without knowing it was a choice.

## How the languages were split

A language takes its phrases whole from CLDR when CLDR's own "N units ago" and "in N units" cannot be built from one past template, one future template and one unit word per plural form. That happens when the unit takes a different grammatical case in each direction (Czech "před 5 minutami" but "za 5 minut"), or when the phrase changes shape (Arabic drops the count for one and two; Swahili writes the unit before the count). Fourteen languages are in that group: Arabic, Czech, Finnish, Hungarian, Armenian, Icelandic, Georgian, Kazakh, Lithuanian, Slovak, Slovenian, Albanian, Serbian and Swahili. Nine more take their phrases, and their durations, whole from CLDR because CLDR is the library's only source for them: Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu. Their data is generated by `tools/cldr/relative-time.mjs` and checked against a generated fixture by `RelativeTimeCldrTest`.

Every other language keeps a compact form in `RelativeTime.resources.kt`: a past template, a future template, and unit words by plural form. In that group, unit words that were wrong were corrected from CLDR. The connecting words were not changed.

## Differences left in place

All of these are in the compact-form group. The library's wording is a valid alternative to CLDR's.

| Language | Library | CLDR |
|---|---|---|
| Spanish, Portuguese (future) | en 2 horas / em 2 horas | dentro de 2 horas |
| Romanian (future) | în 2 ore | peste 2 ore |
| Greek (past) | πριν 2 ώρες | πριν από 2 ώρες |
| Azerbaijani | 2 saat əvvəl / 2 saat sonra | 2 saat öncə / 2 saat ərzində |
| Hindi (future) | 2 घंटे बाद | 2 घंटे में |
| Hindi (month) | महीना | माह |
| Persian (future) | 2 ساعت دیگر | 2 ساعت بعد |
| Malay (past) | 2 jam yang lalu | 2 jam lalu |
| Vietnamese (future) | 2 giờ nữa | sau 2 giờ nữa |
| Norwegian Bokmål and Nynorsk (day) | 2 dager / 2 dagar | 2 døgn |
| Thai | อีก 2ชั่วโมง | ในอีก 2 ชั่วโมง |
| Korean (month) | 2달 전 | 2개월 전 |
| Japanese (month) | 2ヶ月前 | 2 か月前 |
| Chinese (second, minute) | 2秒前 | 2秒钟前 |
| Macedonian (hour) | 2 часови | 2 часа |
| Hebrew | לפני 2 ימים | לפני יומיים |

Japanese, Traditional Chinese and Thai also differ from CLDR in whether a space separates the count from the unit.

## Two special cases

**Hebrew** would qualify for whole phrases by the rule above, because CLDR writes one and two as words without a digit. It was left in the compact form, which always shows the digit, because the CLDR data for Hebrew is not consistent from unit to unit in the release this was checked against.

**Swahili** is generated from CLDR with one adjustment: CLDR capitalises "Sekunde" in the past form and no other unit, and the library writes it in lower case like the rest. The adjustment is recorded in the generator.

## When to revisit

- A user of one of these languages reports that the library's wording reads as wrong, not merely different.
- The output has to match a platform formatter exactly, for example to sit beside text the platform produced.
- A later CLDR release settles the Hebrew data, or changes one of the wordings above.

To move a language to CLDR's wording, add it to `LANGUAGES` in `tools/cldr/relative-time.mjs`, run the generator, and remove its hand-written unit table. The fixture test then covers it.

## Not covered here

The "Recently" and "Shortly" labels: CLDR has nothing to compare them with, so they are the library's own wording in every language. `RelativeStyle.Short` takes CLDR's short phrases outside English and is not part of this comparison, which is of the long phrases; English keeps its own short form ("5m"). Inuktitut has no CLDR relative-time data and was not part of the comparison.
