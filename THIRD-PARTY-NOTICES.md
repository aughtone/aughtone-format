## Unicode CLDR

Values derived from the Unicode Common Locale Data Repository (CLDR), published by Unicode, Inc., are compiled into the published artifact in these places:

- `List.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — the locale list-join patterns.
- `RelativeDayLabels.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — the words for today, tomorrow and yesterday.
- `RelativeTimePatterns.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — the relative-time phrases for twenty-three languages, and the short relative-time phrases for every bundled language but English and Inuktitut, generated by `tools/cldr/relative-time.mjs`; and in `RelativeTime.resources.kt`, corrected unit words for a further eleven.
- `DurationPatterns.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — the duration phrases for nine South Asian languages, the short duration phrases for every bundled language but English and Inuktitut, and each language's word for "now", generated by `tools/cldr/relative-time.mjs`.
- `PluralCategory.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — the plural rules for whole numbers.
- `Duration.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — the duration unit words for Afrikaans, Dutch, Indonesian, Malay, Swahili, Azerbaijani, Slovenian, Latvian and Romanian.
- `Number.resources.kt` and `Money.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — which decimal and grouping separators a language uses, and on which side it writes a currency symbol.
- `LocaleDatePatterns.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — the date patterns for English outside North America and for French in Canada.
- `LocaleClockHoursSource.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — which locales write the time on a 12-hour clock.
- `LocaleInDateNames.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — the month and weekday names used inside a date for twelve languages, generated by `tools/cldr/format-names.mjs`.
- `WindowsZoneMapping.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — the mapping from Windows time-zone names to IANA identifiers, and the zones' aliases, generated by `tools/cldr/windows-zones.mjs`.
- `JapaneseEras.kt` (in the `:calendars` module) — the Japanese era list, generated by `tools/cldr/calendars.mjs`.
- `LocaleIntervalFormats.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — the date and time interval patterns and interval separators, generated by `tools/cldr/interval-formats.mjs`.
- `LocaleFieldFormats.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — the patterns for a chosen set of fields (CLDR `availableFormats`), the standard date and time patterns, the date-time joins, the appendItems and the field names they use, generated by `tools/cldr/field-formats.mjs`.
- `LocaleMonthNamesSource.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — the abbreviated Chinese month names.

Most localized time-zone names are read from the reader's own platform CLDR at runtime (`java.time`, Android ICU, Apple `NSTimeZone`, JavaScript/Wasm `Intl`) and are not compiled into this artifact.

Source: https://cldr.unicode.org/

CLDR is distributed under the Unicode License (https://www.unicode.org/license.txt). This library reproduces values derived from CLDR to provide localized formatting and claims no rights in the underlying data. Consult the Unicode License for its terms before redistributing the data itself.

## ICU

The Umm al-Qura month lengths in `UmmAlQuraTable.kt` (in the `:calendars` module), for AH 1300 to 1600, are read from the ICU library's Islamic calendar by `tools/cldr/calendars.mjs`. ICU is distributed under the Unicode License (https://www.unicode.org/license.txt).

## Hong Kong Observatory

The Chinese calendar's months in `ChineseTable.kt` (in the `:calendars` module), for 1901 to 2099, are derived from the Hong Kong Observatory's Gregorian–Lunar calendar conversion tables by `tools/calendars/chinese-table.mjs`.

Source: https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/gts/time/conversion1_text.htm

## Inuktut Tusaalanga

The Inuktitut (`iu`) weekday names in `LocaleDayOfWeekNamesSource.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) are taken from the Inuktut Tusaalanga glossary, an Inuktut language resource.

Source: https://tusaalanga.ca/glossary

## First-party curated data — not from CLDR

Data authored for this project is maintained under this project's own copyright and is **not** extracted from CLDR or any other third-party source. This includes, for example, the relative-time phrasings of the languages not listed above and most of the duration phrasings across supported locales, the "Recently" and "Shortly" labels, the Georgian ordinal forms in `Ordinality.resources.kt` (in the `:readable` module) — CLDR has no Georgian ordinal rules — and the Inuktitut time-zone names in `TimeZoneNameSupplement.kt` (in the `:datetime` module) — CLDR carries no Inuktitut time-zone names, so these were curated for this project. It carries no third-party terms.
